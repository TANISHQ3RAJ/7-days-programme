# Instructor Handbook

## OVERVIEW
This handbook provides the instructor with daily speaking points, ice-breaker activities, engagement questions, demo ideas, and tips for handling live Q&A. 

---

## General Tips for Live Teaching
1. **Energy is Everything:** The subject can get technical. Maintain high energy, use analogies, and speak with passion about the "future of AI."
2. **Pacing:** Stick strictly to the 1.5-hour breakdown. Use a timer.
3. **No-Code First:** Emphasize that these tools are accessible to everyone, not just programmers.
4. **Interactive:** Never speak for more than 15 minutes without asking a question or doing a live demo.

---

## DAY 1: Introduction to Generative AI

**Ice-Breaker Activity (5 min):** 
"Type in the chat: What is your biggest fear or biggest excitement about AI?"

**Key Speaking Points:**
- AI is not new, but *Generative AI* is a paradigm shift. We went from AI that *analyzes* data to AI that *creates* data.
- Emphasize the difference between narrow AI (what we have) and AGI (what we are building towards).

**Demo Idea:**
- Open ChatGPT. Ask it a basic factual question. Then ask it to rewrite the answer as a Shakespearean pirate. This perfectly illustrates "Generative" capabilities.

**Common Doubts:**
- *Will AI steal my job?* -> "AI won't replace you. Someone using AI will replace you."
- *Is it safe to use?* -> Discuss basic data privacy (not putting sensitive info into free tiers).

**Student Engagement Question:**
"If you could have an AI automate one task in your daily life, what would it be?"

---

## DAY 2: Prompt Engineering Fundamentals

**Ice-Breaker Activity:**
"Can someone give me a bad prompt they used recently that gave them a terrible answer?"

**Key Speaking Points:**
- A prompt is just giving the AI context, an instruction, and an expected output format.
- "Garbage in, garbage out" is the golden rule of LLMs.
- Explain the concept of the "Persona" (e.g., Act as a Senior Marketing Executive).

**Demo Idea:**
- **Zero-shot:** "Write an email to my boss asking for a raise." (Result: generic).
- **Few-shot:** Show examples of specific emails you like, then ask it to write the email. (Result: highly tailored).

**Common Doubts:**
- *Do I need to learn to code to be a prompt engineer?* -> No, you need to learn how to think logically and communicate clearly.

---

## DAY 3: Context Engineering (MAIN HIGHLIGHT)

**Ice-Breaker Activity:**
"How many of you have hit the 'Context Limit Reached' error in ChatGPT?"

**Key Speaking Points:**
- Prompts are the steering wheel; Context is the map. If the AI doesn't have the right map, it will drive into a wall (hallucinate).
- RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) is just a fancy term for giving the AI an open book to read from before it answers the test.

**Demo Idea:**
- Open NotebookLM. Upload a complex PDF (like a research paper). Ask it questions. Show how it cites its sources. This is a massive 'Aha!' moment for students.

**Common Doubts:**
- *What's a token?* -> Think of a token as a syllable or a piece of a word. 100 tokens is roughly 75 words.

---

## DAY 4: AI Tools Ecosystem

**Ice-Breaker Activity:**
"Besides ChatGPT, what AI tools are you currently using?"

**Key Speaking Points:**
- Don't get attached to one tool. The landscape changes weekly. Focus on the *use case*.
- Compare Open-Source (Hugging Face, Llama) vs Closed-Source (OpenAI, Anthropic).

**Demo Idea:**
- Generate a presentation live using Gamma.app from a single prompt. Students usually go crazy for this.

**Student Engagement Question:**
"Who here has tried to make an AI image and ended up with something terrifying?"

---

## DAY 5: AI Automation & Agents

**Ice-Breaker Activity:**
"What is the most boring, repetitive task you have to do every week?"

**Key Speaking Points:**
- An agent is an AI that has agency—it can *do* things, not just talk. It has tools (like web search, calculator, email access).
- Multi-agent systems are like a company: a CEO agent, a Researcher agent, a Writer agent, all talking to each other.

**Demo Idea:**
- Open n8n or Flowise. Show a visual representation of an AI workflow. Connect a trigger (receive email) to an action (AI summarizes it).

**Common Doubts:**
- *Is this coding?* -> It's visual logic. If you can use a flowchart, you can build this.

---

## DAY 6 & 7: Projects & Future Careers

**Key Speaking Points:**
- Building projects is the only way to actually learn. Don't get stuck in "tutorial hell."
- Emphasize that adding "AI Tools" to a resume is no longer special. Adding "Built a custom RAG pipeline for customer support" is what gets you hired.

**Demo Idea:**
- Show a polished, final version of the "AI Study Assistant" project.

**Wrap-up:**
Make the final day celebratory. Encourage networking and emphasize the community aspect.
